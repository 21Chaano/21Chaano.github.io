In education, AI is seen as both a blessing and a curse. To some students, AI tools are the silver bullet to all their problems. To other students, AI is the best study buddy they have ever had. To some instructors, AI is problematic to a student’s learning. To other instructors, AI is a useful tool for students to experiment with. Regardless of what any of these people think, AI is the future of everything. In software development, AI is like your handy-dandy screwdriver in a mechanic’s belt. AI can be a swiss army knife for a programmer, from efficient programming to quick problem solving. Granted, the programmer has to be able to use their AI tools properly.

Throughout this semester, I have used AI tools for all my classes, whether it was asking for help to approach a problem, helping me compile notes on various research papers and articles, or in the case of ICS314, write code. The tools I relied on where ChatGPT for problem debugging and looking for ideas, and BlackBox for efficient coding within my programming environment.

## It's Nothing Personal

There are many assignments and experiences throughout the course of this semester, most of which involved me using AI. In the beginning, I tried my best to not use AI, even when I was slamming my face into the wall on problems that proved to be too challenging for me. Outside of the class, I would use it all willy-nilly just to play around or if I was too lazy to do a google search.

<ins>WODs to Workout Your Brain and Fingers</ins>

Workouts of the Day, or WODs, are weekly coding challenges that are aimed to help us practice coding under pressure. In the first few weeks of the semester, I tried my best not to use AI, as I felt my pride as a programmer was on the line. However, I hit a point where I realized that it was okay to rely on AI to help point me in the right direction. The first time I used ChatGPT to help me with a WOD was on one of the functional programming WODs, Jamba Juice. We had to use functional programming to make classes and functions that would simulate placing orders and managing inventory. I attempted the problem myself at first, but then I hit a brick wall. I took the sledge hammer known as ChatGPT and slammed that into the problem, allowing me to finish on time. In successive WODs, I would on-again off-again use ChatGPT whenever I got stuck.

<ins>WODs Sparring with AI</ins>

While I never showed up to any of the practice WODs in class, the take-home WODs were actually graded. At first, I completed these practice WODs and did not care whether or not I used my AI tools. However, at later points in the semester, I would open the assignment, throw the problem into ChatGPT, and use whatever answer it gives me. Of course, I would actually check to see if the code worked, then fix it myself if necessary, but I was more liberal with using AI when it came to taking the WOD home.

<ins>WODs Fighting AI</ins>

As mentioned earlier, I tried not to use AI in the in-class WODs out of sheer stubbornness. Looking back on it, I should have just used it whenever I got stuck for too long. That is especially the case since I am on a time limit to get the final solution. Maybe about midway through the semester, I had stopped asking ChatGPT to give me code and instead used it to decipher error messages, since I had already recognized patterns and was able to write the code myself. All in all though, I really should not have felt so guilty or stubborn to use AI.

<ins>Having a Way With Words</ins>

When it comes to writing my essays, I do not use any AI tools… for this class. Generally, writing technical essays for ICS314 is fairly easy and straightforward. Just write my mind. In other classes however, I would use ChatGPT to write my essays for me because I either was on an insane time crunch, felt my efforts were better placed on various projects, or just did not feel like writing. If I did end up writing an essay on my own, I would instead use ChatGPT and Grammarly to fix up any grammar mistakes or change the tone of my writing. Come to think of it, it would be kind of funny if this essay was written using AI.

<ins>The Cherry on Top of the Class</ins>

While working on the final project for this class, I did rely on AI several times. I used AI to help frame the stylesheets or to debug error messages. I also used BlackBox to auto-code text based on patterns for more efficient coding. My partners also had their fair share of using AI to help write the code to get the web app working. As a whole, we did mostly rely on our own skill, rather than having AI do the work for us. However, one specific example of having to rely on the AI to solve our problems was the implementation of cookies for an agreement page. We could not figure out how to do this, so ChatGPT solved the problem for us.

<ins>Answer the Question</ins>

When it comes to answering questions in class, I just did not. I had no need to answer questions using AI since I never really answered any questions I did not have answers to in class. No answer? No AI.

<ins>Ask and You Shall Receive</ins>

In class, we learned about smart questions. Any question I needed to ask was made by myself. I never needed ChatGPT to help me format my question. Rather, it would be more like I used smart questions to weasel answers out of ChatGPT.

<ins>Show me Your Code</ins>

One example of me using AI to code was for the navbars in the UI design WODs. I had a particularly tough time figuring out how to code the navbar, so I threw the requirements into AI and it gave me the template of the answer, where I filled and fixed the rest.

<ins>Tell it to me Straight</ins>

While I did not use AI to explain code in this class, I did for another. I am a TA for ECE160, a beginner’s programming class for engineers. I would often use AI to explain the answer key’s code or a student's code so I would know how to help them fix their code.

<ins>Write me a Story</ins>

Like discussed earlier, I used ChatGPT and BlackBox to write code a lot. It was often quick and easy, though sometimes the AI’s solution was way too complex.

<ins>Have You Read the README?</ins>

The good thing about AI-generated code is that it will give some comments already. I would also use AI to explain the code then add comments to better understand code at a glance.


<ins>This Look Good to You?</ins>

The most I did for QA in coding was asking why certain lines gave errors, or if my process was correct when coding.

<ins>If "Other", Please Clarify Here</ins>

There are not many other uses I can think of here for AI in ICS314. I feel that all the things I have used it for have already been covered above.

## Did You *Really* Learn?

One of the advantages of AI is that you can use it to better learn and understand things. However, an over reliance on it can actually harm your learning. In the weeks we were supposed to have been learning functional programming, I had given up and let ChatGPT do the WODs for me. As a result, I did not learn how to actually write code that uses functional programming in TypeScript. Although personally, I feel like it is not much of a loss on my part, considering software development is not what I want to be doing in the future. In other situations, I feel I have been able to understand a topic somewhat better thanks to AI. Physics is one of my weaker subjects. In my electromagnetics class, I used AI to help me understand how to approach different problems, leading me to better understand the process and why it works.

## A Day in the Life of AI

AI has many uses outside of the classroom. AI is used across various industries like healthcare, where AI powers diagnostic tools. Businesses use AI for analytics, customer support through chatbots, and automating tasks, which in turn improves efficiency and decision-making. In transportation, AI is used in self-driving cars and optimized traffic management systems. It also enhances education through personalized learning platforms and adaptive tutoring. Creative industries are also using AI for content generation, from music composition to visual design.

## Compare and Contrast

Traditional teaching methods in software engineering education, such as lectures, textbooks, and static assignments, often provide basic knowledge but might not be very adaptable to individual learning paces and styles. On the other hand, AI-enhanced tools like intelligent tutoring systems, code analysis platforms, and adaptive learning environments offer personalized feedback and real-time guidance. These technologies enhance engagement by simulating real-world coding scenarios and gamifying learning, which can be more motivating for students. AI also supports knowledge retention through adaptive repetition, ensuring students focus on weaker areas. Practical skill development benefits significantly from AI-powered code review tools and automated debugging systems, which help students learn from their mistakes more efficiently than traditional methods. While traditional methods offer consistency and a human element, AI-enhanced approaches can complement them by fostering an interactive, tailored, and skill-oriented learning experience.

## Moving Toward the Future

The future of AI in software engineering education promises transformative advancements, integrating cutting-edge technologies to enhance learning and skill acquisition. AI-driven platforms could offer even more sophisticated adaptive learning systems, tailoring curricula to individual students' progress and career goals. Virtual tutors and AI-enhanced coding environments may simulate complex, real-world software engineering challenges, providing hands-on experience in a controlled setting. However, challenges remain, including the need for high-quality training data to prevent bias, ensuring AI tools are accessible to diverse student populations, and addressing concerns over replacing human instructors' mentorship. Additionally, fostering collaboration between AI systems and educators will be critical to create hybrid learning models that balance technological efficiency with the empathy and contextual understanding of human teaching. Continuous improvement in AI ethics, data privacy, and equitable access will be essential to realize its full potential in reshaping software engineering education.

## Concluding Thoughts

In conclusion, the integration of AI into education, particularly in software engineering, highlights both its potential and its challenges. AI serves as a powerful tool for enhancing learning experiences, offering personalized guidance, fostering practical skills, and optimizing problem-solving. However, as seen throughout this semester, reliance on AI must be balanced with active learning and critical thinking to ensure a deeper understanding of the subject matter. The future of AI in education is promising, with opportunities to revolutionize how we approach teaching and learning. Yet, it requires careful navigation to address accessibility, ethics, and the human element in education. Ultimately, AI is not just a tool for efficiency but a companion in the journey of learning—its value depends on how thoughtfully and responsibly it is used.
